The Structure of Mathematical Reality

a self-study curriculum  ·  seventeen chapters  ·  cc by-sa 4.0

A self-study curriculum on what mathematics is, what it does when it works, and why it connects to the physical world at all.

Mathematics is not one subject but a family of self-contained systems, each built from its own axioms, with its own internal logic and its own blind spots. Call them canvases. Following that idea surfaced the question the whole curriculum is built around: why do some mathematical tools feel disproportionately powerful, a few moves producing exact and sweeping results, while others grind through brute computation for a comparable payoff?

Seventeen chapters move from the standard toolkits toward that structural question. This is aimed at people who already have some mathematical fluency but want the connective tissue between fields, the “why does this work here and not there” layer. It is a map, not a textbook, and where a question is genuinely open it says so. I built it to study from myself and thought it might be useful to others.

~/method

Each chapter is a self-contained unit meant to be worked through in one intensive sitting rather than on a fixed weekly schedule, and runs in parallel with outside reading. The spine is the Princeton Companion to Mathematics; each chapter’s reading names the exact sections, plus the supplements (Weyl, Carter, Axler, Strogatz, MacKay, Lawvere and Schanuel, and a number of papers). Every chapter carries seven documents:

documentwhat it is
readingWhat to read alongside the chapter, and how far to get
lectureThe core prose: introduces and develops the ideas
slidesThe lecture compressed to its skeleton, for review
notesA structured template you fill in yourself
exercisesShort problems that test whether the idea landed
assignmentProduce something that demonstrates real understanding
quizApplication and explanation, not recall

Progress on the map above is remembered by your browser alone: no account, no tracking, nothing leaves this page. Mark a chapter done at the end of its lecture and the map fills in.
